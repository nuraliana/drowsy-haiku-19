# Candidates — Seventeen Syllables of Debugging

All haiku are 3 lines, 5-7-5 syllables, 17 total, about debugging.
Syllable counts are per line. Scores are out of 25 against the judge
criteria: debugging haiku form, concrete fresh middle image, turn
between lines 2 and 3, every word earning its place, clean read-aloud rhythm.

## 1. Breakpoint / lying logs

> The breakpoint sleeps on (5)
> while logs insist nothing moved (7)
> the variable changed (5)

**Score: 23/25.** Strong, concrete turn: the logs claim stillness, then
the variable itself contradicts them. "sleeps on" is slightly soft and
the first line leans on personification rather than a sharper image.

## 2. CHAMPION — The counter that moved itself

> Midnight, the counter (5)
> holds one more than I left it (7)
> no one touched the code (5)

**Score: 24/25.** The middle line is concrete and fresh — a counter
holding one more than it was left with, a variable changing itself.
The last line reframes the whole scene as a quiet mystery: nobody
touched anything, yet something moved. Every word earns its place;
it reads aloud like plain speech, with a calm, eerie turn.

## 3. The log that says all clear

> The log says all clear (5)
> each line stamped, calm, and certain (7)
> the crash left no trace (5)

**Score: 23/25.** A lying-log image with a clean turn: certainty on
paper, no trace of the crash. The middle line is a touch list-like
("calm, and certain"), which costs a point against the champion.

## 4. The rubber duck

> The rubber duck stares (5)
> as I explain the loop aloud (7)
> the flaw says my name (5)

**Score: 22/25.** The turn lands well — explaining aloud, the flaw
turns out to be mine. But rubber-duck debugging is a familiar trope,
so the middle image is less fresh than the champion's.

## 5. The stale comment

> Old comments still swear (5)
> the function does what it should (7)
> the code has moved on (5)

**Score: 21/25.** Concrete angle (an expired comment) and a real turn:
the promise stayed, the code left. "does what it should" is a little
abstract in the middle, and the rhythm is flatter than the champion's.

## 6. The trap stepped past

> I set the trap twice (5)
> the code stepped past, unbothered (7)
> the bug just waved back (5)

**Score: 22/25.** Playful and concrete — a breakpoint stepped past,
a bug waving back. The turn is charming, but "just" is doing light
filler work and the tone leans on cleverness over image.

## 7. The test that passed at home

> The test passed at home (5)
> but fails where the grader looks (7)
> my machine lied twice (5)

**Score: 21/25.** A real, specific debugging pain (passes locally,
fails elsewhere). "lied twice" is an odd claim — once would be the
lie — and the last line explains rather than reframes.

## 8. Print statements

> Print statements bloom here (5)
> each line confesses anew (7)
> the answer hides still (5)

**Score: 19/25.** The middle image is concrete, but "hides still" is
awkward phrasing chosen for the count, and the turn does not reframe
anything — it only restates that the answer is hidden.

## Winner

Candidate 2, score 24/25 — the highest, and above the 24 minimum.
